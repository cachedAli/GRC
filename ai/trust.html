AI trust & control

AI suggests. People decide. You can see every call.

How the AI is governed: what it only suggests and what it does on its own, how prompts are masked before they leave your server, the audit trail of every call, and the budgets administrators track.

Book a live demo →The data-protection gateway

Suggests or automatic

Suggestion is the default. Of the 39 capabilities we list, 31 suggest and wait for a person. The 8 that run on their own say so: they read, score or write observations, and the links they make can be locked or rejected.

  • Suggestions a person confirms: evidence-to-requirement links, access-review decisions, vendor narratives (the governed score comes from the scoring engine), audit observations, document details, studio edits, workflow definitions.
  • Automatic, and reversible: policy statements mapped to controls (locked rows survive re-runs); findings linked to controls at or above a confidence floor (a Reject removes them, weaker ones wait).
  • Automatic scores and text: the evidence quality score, dashboard observations, and circular reading with unverified quotes flagged.

The data-protection gateway

Masked before it leaves your server. The gateway runs before a prompt leaves the server. If the protection check itself fails, the call is refused and the original prompt is never sent: it fails closed, and the screen says plainly that AI was not used.

Removed

Never sent, never restored

  • Secrets
  • Card numbers
  • Account numbers
  • Identity numbers
  • MAC addresses

Masked

Swapped for placeholders, put back in the answer

  • E-mail addresses
  • Phone numbers
  • IP addresses
  • Cloud IDs
  • Host names
  • People
  • Organisations
  • Units
  • Vendors
  • Projects
  • Assets

Left alone

Public names the model needs

  • Frameworks
  • Regulators
  • Common products
  • Role titles

Refused

Not sent at all

  • Images and scanned pages (read by OCR on your server first)
  • Use cases registered as never-send
  • A prompt that still holds a secret after masking

Before · on your server

Revoke VPN access for mark.allen@example-bank.sa on core-db-02 (10.20.4.17). Card 4111 1111 1111 1111 is on file.

After · what the model sees

Revoke VPN access for [EMAIL_1] on [HOST_1] ([IP_1]). Card removed is on file.

Masked, then put back in the answerRemoved, never sent or restored

  • Your own names are known. The tenant’s people, units, vendors and assets are recognised by name, including in Arabic text.
  • Each feature declares its fields. Owners, internal IDs and raw scanner output are left out; a new column is never sent until it is classified.
  • Images stay on your server. Scanned pages are read by OCR locally; an image that cannot be read is refused, never sent to an outside AI.
  • No tracer sees the prompt. External tracing is switched off, because a tracer would see prompts before the gateway.

What it does not cover. Eight registered use cases do not pass through the gateway today, one optional path in the AI risk assessment among them. We say “AI prompts pass through a data-protection gateway”, never “every AI call”.

Audit trail

Every AI call, on the record.

  • A row per call: who asked (or which job), module, feature, use case, model, tokens, estimated cost, status and duration.
  • The sanitised text, encrypted. The prompt and answer after masking are kept encrypted for 30 days by default.
  • Reading it is a right, and logged. Viewing the log and reading a prompt’s text are separate permissions, and opening the text writes its own audit row.
  • Blocked calls count too. A refused call is recorded with the status “blocked”.
  • Accepted suggestions are ordinary edits. When a person accepts one, the change goes through the platform’s audit trail like any other: who, when, old value, new value.
